Rhythm & Counting

Music moves in time. Learn the steady beat, how measures group those beats, and what the notes on the staff are asking you to do.

The pulse

Beat

The steady tick you feel — like a heartbeat. Tap your foot and you’re already counting time.

A group of beats

Measure

Beats live in little boxes. Bar lines mark the walls. Each box is one measure.

Say the numbers

Counting

In 4/4 time, count 1 · 2 · 3 · 4 and start again. Beat 1 is the strongest.

One measure of 4/4 · count out loud

The treble clef curls around the second line from the bottom — that’s G. The 4/4 means four quarter-note beats in every measure.

Time signature

What 4/4 means

44

Top number — how many beats in each measure.
Bottom number — which note gets one beat (4 = quarter note).

Common time (𝔠) is another way to write 4/4.

Bar lines close each measure. A double bar often ends a section.

Staff secrets

Treble clef

Also called the G clef. It tells you which notes live where on the five lines.

Bar line = end of a measure. Rest = silence for that many beats. Quiet counts too!

Note values · how long each shape lasts in 4/4

4 beats

Half

2 beats

Open note head + stem.
